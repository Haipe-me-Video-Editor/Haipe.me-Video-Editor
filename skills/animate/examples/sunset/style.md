# Style bible: why the sky turns orange at sunset

## Palette
Paper #f3ede2 for words and small marks. Noon sky #4f93c8 to #c4e2ee, sea #2a5f8c to #173a5c. Night ink #1b1a2e
for the diagram. Sun #ffd66e at noon, #ffb347 at sunset. Sunset sky #3a2a5a, #8e3b5a, #c8442f, #f08a3c, #ffcf7a.
Scattered light is always blue #78c2ff.

## Shape and line
Flat shapes, no outlines, round ends on every stroke. Light travels as short waves; air molecules are plain dots.

## Texture and light
Paper fibre over every shot (multiply, 10 to 16 percent), soft vignette, light film grain. Glows are soft radial
falls, never hard rings.

## Motion
Slow and continuous: the sun drifts, the sea shimmers, molecules jiggle. The camera holds in every shot. Shots
cross-fade over 0.35 s.

## Words on screen
One line per shot, Instrument Serif Italic 92 px, centred near the top, rising in over 0.7 s. A small mono credit
at the end.

## Never
Words over the sun or the subject. Hard black outlines. A colour outside the palette.

## Facts to get right
Air molecules scatter short wavelengths far more than long ones (Rayleigh scattering, strength goes with 1/wavelength
to the fourth). At sunset the light reaches you through much more atmosphere than at noon, so most of the blue is
scattered out on the way and the light that arrives is orange and red.
